## Pairing

Work in pairs, with one laptop open: the **driver** types, and the **copilot**
reads ahead, suggests what to try, and catches mistakes. Both of you say what
you're thinking. This is how your
[pairing sessions with a TA](/syllabus#pair-programming-sessions) work, with you
as the driver. Attendance is taken (see [Labs](/syllabus#course-structure)).

own file, so you switch laptops too:

1. Before you start, each of you opens your
   [scratch repository](/class/2#your-scratch-repository) in vscode.dev, and

## A Day of Talks

A day of talks is a list of the talks' lengths, in minutes, in the order they
are given, one right after another, from minute 0: `[30, 15, 45]` is a
30-minute talk, then a 15-minute one, then a 45-minute one.

## Problem 1

Design `start_times`, which gives the minute at which each talk starts:
`start_times([30, 15, 45])` is `[0, 30, 45]`. Write it with a `for` loop, with
each accumulator's statement as a comment where it starts, as in
[Class 17](/class/17).

## Problem 2

Design `start_times_rec`, the same function as `start_times`, with a recursive
helper and an accumulator instead of a loop, as in [Class 31](/class/31). Then,
in a comment, say where each part of the accumulator is in both versions: its
statement, its first value, and its next value.

## Problem 3

Design `count_rises`, which takes a list of daily temperatures, and gives how
many days were warmer than the day before. The first day has no day before it,
so it isn't counted. Use a recursive helper with an accumulator: what does it
need to know about the days already passed?

## The Phone Tree, Again

The phone tree of [Lab 5](/lab/5):

```python
@dataclass
class NoOne:
    pass

@dataclass
class Caller:
    name: str
    team: str
    joined: int
    first: PhoneTree
    second: PhoneTree

# A PhoneTree is who gets a call: no one, or a Caller, who joined in the year
# `joined`, and then calls the two PhoneTrees `first` and `second`.
PhoneTree = NoOne | Caller

kai = Caller("Kai", "red", 2021, NoOne(), NoOne())
lena = Caller("Lena", "blue", 2019, NoOne(), NoOne())
mo = Caller("Mo", "red", 2023, kai, lena)
nia = Caller("Nia", "blue", 2024, NoOne(), NoOne())
oren = Caller("Oren", "red", 2018, mo, nia)
```

## Problem 4

Design `newer_than_caller`, which gives the names of the people in a phone tree
who joined later than the person who called them: a caller's name before the
names in their `first` tree, and those before the names in their `second` tree.
The person at the top of the tree wasn't called by anyone, so they aren't
counted. What does each call need to know from above
([Class 32](/class/32))?

## Problem 5

Design `count_joined_before`, which takes a phone tree and a year, and gives how
many people in it joined before that year. Compare it with `count_team`, from
[Lab 5](/lab/5)'s Problem 1. Then abstract the two into `count_callers`, which
takes a function that says whether to count a `Caller`, and a phone tree, and
gives how many people in the tree it says to count, as in
[Class 27](/class/27). Write `count_team` and `count_joined_before` as calls of
it. Is `count_callers` generic? Why, or why not?
