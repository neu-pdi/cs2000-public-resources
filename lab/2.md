## Pairing

Work in pairs, with one laptop open: the **driver** types, and the **copilot**
reads ahead, suggests what to try, and catches mistakes. Both of you say what
you're thinking. This is how your
[pairing sessions with a TA](/syllabus#pair-programming-sessions) work, with you
as the driver. Attendance is taken (see [Labs](/syllabus#course-structure)).

Switch roles at each problem. The driver works on their own laptop, in their
own file, so you switch laptops too:

1. Before you start, each of you opens your
   [scratch repository](/class/2#your-scratch-repository) in vscode.dev, and
   makes a new file, `lab2.py`. Its first line should be `#level beginner`. Put
   the line from [Checking your tests](#checking-your-tests) under it.
2. Problems 2 and 3 use the potholes table and `z02115`, from Problem 1. Before
   you drive one, copy them into your file, if your partner made them (they can
   send them to you).
3. At the end, commit and push your file. Each of you has the problems you
   drove; if you'd like the whole lab, send each other your files.

## Potholes

Boston's "Street Bump" data has a row for each pothole, with its neighborhood,
zip code, latitude, and longitude: load it from
`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/neu-pdi/cs2000-public-resources/refs/heads/main/static/support/potholes_2024.csv`
(and convert the latitudes and longitudes to numbers). Plan each of Problems 1
to 3 first, naming the template for each step.

## Problem 1

Make a table, `z02115`, of just the potholes in zip code `02115`, and show it
with `scatter_plot`, longitude across and latitude up. (Up close, latitude and
longitude work as coordinates on a flat map.)

## Problem 2

Find the southernmost of those potholes, by sorting `z02115` by latitude.

## Problem 3

Add a new column, `midpt`, to `z02115`: the average of each pothole's latitude
and longitude, with a helper, `mid_point`, on a row.

## Problem 4

Potholes are reported through 311, Boston's number for non-emergency problems,
and, since 2023, found too from anonymized data sent by cars
([Automated Pothole Detection](https://www.boston.gov/departments/analytics-team/automated-pothole-detection-0)).
If you wanted every neighborhood's potholes to get the same attention, why
wouldn't the 311 reports alone be enough? How do the cars' data help? Answer in
two or three sentences, in a comment.
