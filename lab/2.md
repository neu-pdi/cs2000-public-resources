## Potholes

Boston's "Street Bump" data has a row for each pothole, with its neighborhood,
zip code, latitude, and longitude: load it from
`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/neu-pdi/cs2000-public-resources/refs/heads/main/static/support/potholes_2024.csv`
(and convert the latitudes and longitudes to numbers). Plan each of Problems 1
to 3 first, naming the template for each step.

## Problem 1

Make a table, `z02115`, of just the potholes in zip code `02115`, and show it
with `scatter_plot`, longitude across and latitude up. (Up close, latitude and
longitude work as coordinates on a flat map.)

## Problem 2

Find the southernmost of those potholes, by sorting `z02115` by latitude.

## Problem 3

and longitude, with a helper, `mid_point`, on a row.

## Problem 4

Potholes are reported through 311, Boston's number for non-emergency problems,
and, since 2023, found too from anonymized data sent by cars
([Automated Pothole Detection](https://www.boston.gov/departments/analytics-team/automated-pothole-detection-0)).
If you wanted every neighborhood's potholes to get the same attention, why
wouldn't the 311 reports alone be enough? How do the cars' data help? Answer in
two or three sentences, in a comment.

## Problem 7

In 2012, Target's data team used purchases like unscented lotion and prenatal
vitamins to predict which customers were pregnant, and mailed coupons for baby
items to their homes. In one home, as the
[New York Times reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2012/02/19/magazine/shopping-habits.html),
that is how a high-school student's father found out. This is
**data inference**: using pieces of information to infer another, which can
then flow where it shouldn't. Here is the privacy analysis, with the unintended
recipients left blank:

| Question | Answer |
| -- | -- |
| What type of information is shared? | Coupons with the names and prices of products, chosen from past purchases and Target's inferences about the customer's stage of life and needs |
| Who is the subject of the information? | A Target customer |
| Who is the sender of the information? | Target |
| Who are the potential recipients of the information? | **Intended:** the customer<br/>**Unintended:** _to be filled in_ |
| What principles govern the collection and transmission of the information? | Information is collected from Target's purchase records and bought from data brokers. No consent was given for the collection, or for the transmission. |

In comments:

1. Name two unintended recipients of the information that this customer is
   likely to be pregnant: one from the story, and at least one other kind.
2. How could Target change the way it sends coupons, to have fewer unintended
   recipients?

chart show that the other doesn't?
