## Purpose

Get your setup working, start pair programming, and practice with numbers,
strings, and images.

## Pairing

Work in pairs, with **only one laptop open**: the **driver** types, and the **copilot**
reads ahead, suggests what to try, and catches mistakes. Both of you say what
you're thinking. Switch roles at each problem. This is how your
[pairing sessions with a TA](/syllabus#pair-programming-sessions) work, with you
as the driver. Attendance is taken (see [Labs](/syllabus#course-structure)).

## Setting Up

Before you pair, each of you checks your own setup. The full steps are in
[Class 1](/class/1#setting-up) (VS Code and PLL) and
[Class 2](/class/2#your-class-work-folder) (your class work folder); ask a TA
if you get stuck.

1. Open your `cs2000` folder in VS Code (**File → Open Folder...**). If you
   don't have one yet, make it, as in Class 2.
2. In the Extensions sidebar (the four squares), check that
   **Python Language Levels** is installed. Microsoft's **Python** extension
   should not be.
3. Make a new file, `lab0.py`. Its first line should be `#level beginner`.
4. Add `1 + 1` on the next line, and click **PLL: Run Python File**: the
   interactions panel should show `2`. (If the panel doesn't open, run
   **PLL: Show Interactions** from the Command Palette.)

Then decide who drives first. The driver works on their own laptop, in their own
`lab0.py`, so at each problem you switch laptops too. Problem 4 uses Problem 3's
jar: before you drive it, copy the jar into your file, if your partner drew it
(they can send it to you). At the end, each of you has the problems you
drove, in your own file; if you'd like the whole lab, send each other your
files.

## Problem 1

A stall at a farmers market sells honey at \$8 a jar, and jam at \$6.50 a jar.

1. Write an expression for the cost of 3 jars of honey and 2 of jam. Which
   numbers in it are `int`s, and which are `float`s? What type is the total?
2. A customer buys 2 jars of honey and 4 of jam, and pays with a \$50 bill.
   Write an expression for their change.
3. The stall sold 42 jars on Saturday, 35 on Sunday, and 51 on Wednesday. Write
   an expression for the average number sold in a day. What do you get if you
   leave out the parentheses, and why?
4. With `max` and `min`, write one expression for how many more jars were sold
   on the busiest of those days than on the slowest.

## Problem 2

The stall's sign has room for 20 characters on a line.

1. Does `"Fresh Wildflower Honey"` fit? Use `len` to find out. If it doesn't,
   find a shorter name that does.
2. A line of 20 stars makes a border for the sign. Write it with `*` on a
   string, rather than typing all 20.
3. Find `center` in the
   [string methods documentation](https://docs.python.org/3/library/stdtypes.html#string-methods).
   What does `"HONEY".center(20, "*")` give? Check its length with `len`. Then
   center your honey's name from part 1 the same way.

## Problem 3

Draw a jar of honey: a gold rectangle for the jar (try 60 wide and 70 tall),
with a narrower brown rectangle above it for the lid, and a white label in the
middle of the jar with the word on it. (`text("HONEY", 14, "black")` is an image
of the word, in 14-pixel black letters.)

Then put three jars side by side. How much did you have to copy? What would you
have to change to make all three lids red? ([Class 3](/class/3) has a better
way.)

## Problem 4

Draw the stall: an awning of six stripes, alternating red and white, each 20
wide and 30 tall, above a brown counter 120 wide and 60 tall. Then stand a jar
from Problem 3 on the counter, under the awning.
