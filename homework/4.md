[Homework 3](/homework/3) gave you a plan, and a first version that runs. This

finishes the program: by the end, it does everything on
[Homework 3's list](/homework/3#project-2-press-any-key).

### Required pieces

As before: every function by the whole [recipe](/class/recipe), and your
pieces listed at the top of `project.py`, continuing Homework 3's list.

8. **The state as a dataclass** ([Class 20](/class/20)). A dataclass that holds the list,
   and whatever else your program keeps track of (a score, a speed, a mode).
   Change your handlers to match.
9. **A union** ([Class 21](/class/21)). Your program's modes, or the kinds of thing on
   screen, as a union: each variant its own dataclass, with a `match` in at
   least one function.
10. **Recursion on a list** ([Class 22](/class/22)). At least one function on a list in
    the state, written with the list template (`case []` and `case [f, *r]`).
11. **Scope** ([Class 19](/class/19)). No handler changes a global name, but global constant definitions can be used. In comments, for
    one handler, say where each name it uses is found: a parameter, a local
    name, or a global one.
12. **Finished** (Classes [15](/class/15)–[22](/class/22)). Your program does everything on Homework 3's
    list: it moves on its own; it responds to the keyboard and to the mouse; it
    has at least two modes, and at least two kinds of thing on screen; its list
    grows and shrinks; and it has an end, or a way to start over.
13. **Who it leaves out.** Writing, in comment at top of `project.py`: someone your design might
    leave out (someone who can't use a mouse, can't tell red from green, or
    needs more time to react), and one change you made for them.
14. **A piece of your own**, as in Homework 3: one function, in one of the same
    three shapes, for what this homework adds:
    - **a new value** in the state that `to_draw` shows (a level, how many
      things are left, a message that depends on the mode);
    - **a new response** wired into a handler: a key that pauses, a click that
      starts over, a tick that makes things harder;
    - **a new view** of the state: a pause screen over the frozen frame, or a
      different picture for each mode.

    Whichever you pick, it hangs off `to_draw` or one of your handlers, so your
    program calls it rather than just defining it. Its Docstring says what it
    does for your audience.
15. **The description, finished.** Writing, in comment at top of `project.py`: who the program is for,
    what it does for them, how to run it (which file, and which reactor to
    start), and what changed from your plan, and why. Write it for someone who
    has never seen this course: it's how you'll describe the project to anyone
    else.

### In the crit

About 15 minutes. Start by letting someone on the panel use your program, until
it ends or starts over (3 minutes). Then the panel picks several pieces from
your list (from either homework), and you walk through each in the recipe's
order. Questions throughout.
