## Project 1: Drawn from Data (continued)

:::info[Your project folder]

Keep working in your `project1` folder, from [Homework 1](/homework/1#your-project-folder).

:::

[Homework 1](/homework/1) gave you an audience, a picture of one record, a rule
for comparing two, and your data. This homework, the end of Project 1, draws
the pictures from your data's rows, cleans the data, computes what your
audience needs, checks your rule against the data, and finishes what you're
making: one image, drawn by your code.

### Required pieces

As before: every function follows the whole [recipe](/class/recipe), and your
pieces listed at the top of `project.py`, continuing Homework 1's list.

7. **Pictures from rows** ([Class 8](/class/8)):
   - a function from a row to its picture, calling Homework 1's functions;
   - a function that takes in two rows, and calls Homework 1's rule with their
     values;
   - a row function, from a row to `True` or `False`, that makes a decision about the records
     your audience cares about most, used with `filter`;
   - a definition: an image of the three records your audience would look at
     first, side by side.

   Test row functions on rows of a small table of your own, as in
   [Class 8](/class/8).
8. **Clean a column** ([Class 11](/class/11)). Most data has a messy column of text: the
   same category spelled two ways, different capitals, blanks, `"N/A"`. Find
   one in yours, and design a function that cleans one value of it, from the
   fixed set of strings it should hold: an `if` for each, and an `else`. Clean
   the column with `transform_column`, and show it with `freq_bar_chart` before
   and after. If your data has nothing messy, say how you checked as a comment.
9. **New columns** (Classes [9](/class/9)–[10](/class/10)):
   - with `add_column`, a column of each record's band (from piece 2), and a
     column of a score your audience needs, computed from several numbers, with
     a helper;
   - two functions from a table to a table that your project needs (the
     records of one category, say, and the best few by your score), each tested
     on small tables of your own ([Class 10](/class/10)).
10. **Check your rule against the data** (Classes [7](/class/7)–[12](/class/12)). Does your rule, or your
    score, do what you meant? Answer at least two questions about it with your
    tables, their charts, and their columns' `sum`, `mean`, `min`, and `max`.
    For example:
    - Does one category always win?
    - Does your score favor big records (fridges, parks) over small ones?
    - Are the bands as full, or as empty, as their names suggest? e.g., if you have a band called `rare`, is it actually rare?

    Then change one rule, weight, or boundary to fix something you found, and
    show the numbers before and after.
11. **What you're making, finished** ([Class 12](/class/12)). First, a plan: its steps, the
    template for each step, and a wish list of helpers. Then the finished
    product: **one** image, made by your code, for your audience, with the following:
    - a title;
    - a legend: each category's picture, with its name;
    - a few key records your audience might be most interested in seeing, as pictures;
    - at least two charts (charts are images, so `above` and `beside` can place
      them in your overall image);
    - a sentence of numbers about the whole dataset, as text (from its columns'
      `mean` or `max`, say, as in [Class 7](/class/7)).
12. **Privacy, of a flow your project would create** (Classes [6](/class/6) and [10](/class/10)). If what
    you made became a site or an app, what would it learn about the people who
    use it? A buyer's guide that remembers what someone looked at learns what
    they're about to buy, which advertisers want to know; a game that sends
    players to places learns where they go. Analyze one such flow of information with the five
    questions. Then name one risk, and a mitigation. (If your records are public
    figures, what you made can itself be the flow.)
13. **A piece of your own**, as in Homework 1: one function, in one of the same
    three shapes, for what this homework adds:
    - **a new value**, computed from your table, shown in the finished image (a
      count of how many records fall in a band, the best record's score);
    - **a new choice** about which records the finished image shows, or in what
      order;
    - **a new view** of the data: a legend, a chart no other piece asks for, or
      your table sorted by something your audience cares about.

    Whichever you pick, it hangs off the finished image or your report, so your
    project calls it rather than just defining it. Its Docstring says what it
    does for your audience.
14. **The description, finished.** Go dack and update your comment describing 
    the project: who the project is for,
    what it helps them do, what you found, and how to see what you made (which
    file to run, and which definition holds the finished image). Write it for
    someone who has never seen this course: it's how you'll describe the
    project to anyone else.

### In the crit

About 15 minutes. Start with what you made, and what you changed after checking
your rule (3 minutes). Then the panel picks several pieces from your list (from
either homework), and you walk through each in the recipe's order. Questions can interrupt
at any time.
