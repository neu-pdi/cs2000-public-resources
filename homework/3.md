## Project 2: Press Any Key

Over Homeworks 3 and 4, you'll make an interactive program for someone: an
animation, built with a [reactor](/pll/reactors), that responds to what they do
with the keyboard and the mouse. For example:

- a game that helps a young child learn to count, or to find the letters on a
  keyboard;
- a toy that shows how something works (rain filling a reservoir, a line at a
  coffee shop), with controls that change it;
- a guided breathing exercise, paced by a shape that grows and shrinks;
- a game that can be played with just the keyboard, or just the mouse, for
  players who can only use one of them;
- a model of a busy crosswalk, for explaining how its lights are timed.

What you make, and for whom, is up to you. The pieces below are what every
project needs, whatever it is. Choose something you'd be glad to show someone
outside the course.

By the end of Homework 4, your program must do all of these, so plan for them
now:

- it moves on its own, as time passes;
- it responds to the keyboard **and** to the mouse;
- it has at least two modes (waiting, playing, paused, finished);
- it has at least two kinds of thing on screen;
- it keeps a list of things that grows **and** shrinks as it runs;
- it has an end, or a way to start over.

This homework plans the program, and builds a first version that runs.
[Homework 4](/homework/4) finishes it.

### Your project folder {#your-project-folder}

Project 2 goes in a folder of its own, `project2`, inside your
[class work folder](/class/2#your-class-work-folder):

1. In VS Code's Explorer, with your `cs2000` folder open, click the New Folder
   icon, and name the folder `project2`.
2. Make a new file in it, `project.py`. Its first line is `#level beginner`:
   change it to `#level intermediate`, by clicking **PLL: beginner** in the
   status bar.

Your project stays in that folder, and you present it from there: at your
crit, open `project2` itself in VS Code (**File → Open Folder...**) before
you start the crit. [Homework 4](/homework/4) continues in it.

### Required pieces

Most pieces are functions. Where a piece is something else (definitions, or
writing), it says so. Design every function with the whole
[recipe](/class/recipe): its Signature, Docstring, and Examples (in test
functions, one per example), and only then its Body. List your pieces at the top
of `project.py`, each with the functions or lines that make it up, so that your
panel can find them.

1. **Who it's for, and the plan.** Describe in English, as a comment (lines starting with `#`) 
   at the top of your `project.py`: your audience; what
   the program does for them; and your plan for all of it, by the end of
   Homework 4. The plan says what the state holds, which handler changes what,
   and what two or three of its frames show, in words. The plan is a promise:
   Homework 4's comment says what changed, and why.
2. **A first version that runs** ([Class 15](/class/15)). Definitions: the
   reactor itself, wired up and started with `.interact()` — its state,
   including your list of things, `to_draw`, `on_tick`, and a handler each for
   the keyboard and the mouse. Get that much running, in a simple form, before
   the pieces below fill it in: they say how each of those functions has to be
   built, where the list at the top of this page says what the program does by
   the end of Homework 4.
3. **Drawing with a loop** ([Class 16](/class/16)). Your `to_draw` builds the frame with a
   `for` loop over the list in the state.
4. **A loop with two variables that change** ([Class 17](/class/17)). A function that needs
   two variables: for example, the thing nearest the mouse, or the biggest so far.
5. **`map` or `filter`** ([Class 14](/class/14)), somewhere a loop would also work: moving
   every thing, say, or dropping the things that have left the screen.
6. **Handlers, with tests** (Classes [5](/class/5) and [15](/class/15)). Every handler has examples,
   including at least one at an edge (an empty list, the last thing, the edge
   of the screen). At least one helper is used by two handlers. And at least one
   test runs the whole reactor through a few events, with `react` or
   `simulate_trace` (in the [Reactors](/pll/reactors) reference).
7. **A piece of your own.** One function, designed with the whole recipe, in one
   of these shapes — whichever your program needs:
   - **a new value** in the state that `to_draw` shows (a score, a timer, a
     warning before something leaves the screen);
   - **a new response** wired into a handler: a key that changes the speed, a
     click that places something, a tick that adds something new;
   - **a new view** of the list in the state: draw only the things in view, or
     order them by how close they are.

   Whichever you pick, it hangs off `to_draw` or one of your handlers, so your
   program calls it rather than just defining it. Its Docstring says what it
   does for your audience.

### In the crit

About 15 minutes. Start by having the panel tell you how to drive the program (2
minutes). Then the panel picks several pieces from your list, and you walk
through each in the recipe's order: what the data is, what the examples
show, and what else you could have done. Questions throughout.
