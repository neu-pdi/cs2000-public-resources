## Project 3: Talk Me Through It

Over Homeworks 5 and 6, you'll make an interactive conversation: a program that
talks someone through something, one question or choice at a time. For
example:

- a guide that helps someone sort their trash into Boston's bins;
- a helper that points a student to the campus office for their problem;
- a game that guesses what a player is thinking of, in a subject you know well;
- an interactive story;
- a quiz that changes its next question with each answer.

Each step says something and offers two options, and each option leads to
another step, or to an outcome: an answer, an ending, a recommendation. So a
conversation is a tree, like the trees of [Class 25](/class/25). You'll put
yours in a spreadsheet (a CSV file), load it as a table, build the tree from
the table, and run it with `input()`. Each answer is a step down the tree, so a conversation
is a recursive walk: it needs no loop.

What you make, and for whom, is up to you. The pieces below are what every
conversation needs, whatever it is. Your panel will try yours in the crit, so
make it something you're happy to share in class.

This homework puts your conversation's steps in a table, builds it, and runs
it.
[Homework 6](/homework/6) keeps a record of each conversation, and reports on
them.

Commit and push as you work: at your crit, you present what's in the

### Required pieces

Most pieces are functions. Where a piece is something else (writing, or the
CSV file), it says so. Design every function with the whole
[recipe](/class/recipe): its Signature, Docstring, and Examples (in test
functions, one per example), and only then its Body. List your pieces at the top
of `project.py`, each with the functions or lines that make it up, so that your
panel can find them.

1. **Who it's for.** Writing, in `README.md`: your audience; what the
   conversation helps them do, or what it lets them experience; and where its
   content comes from. If it gives facts or advice (which bin, which office),
   they must come from real sources: give links.
2. **The data definition** (Classes [21](/class/21) and [25](/class/25)). Start from this one, and write
   its template, `_conversation_temp`:

   ```python
   @dataclass
   class Outcome:
       text: str

   @dataclass
   class Step:
       text: str
       option_a: str
       a: Conversation
       option_b: str
       b: Conversation

   Conversation = Outcome | Step
   ```

   Then define a small conversation in code, with at least two steps and three
   outcomes, for your examples.
3. **Your conversation**, in `conversation.csv`: at least 15 steps and outcomes
   in all, at least 4 outcomes, and at least one path of 5 steps. One row for
   each step or outcome, with the columns `text`, `option_a`, `next_a`,
   `option_b`, and `next_b`. Row 0 is where the conversation starts. `next_a`
   and `next_b` are the numbers of the rows their options lead to, and `-1` in
   an outcome's row. (Put text that has a comma in it in double quotes.)
4. **Build the tree** (Classes [7](/class/7), [22](/class/22), and [25](/class/25)). Load `conversation.csv` with
   `load_table`, and convert its `next_a` and `next_b` columns to numbers. Then
   design `build`, which takes the table and a row number, and gives the
   conversation that starts at that row. Its examples use a small table you
   make with `table`.
5. **Check the table** ([Class 8](/class/8)):
   - How many rows are outcomes?
   - Is every `next_a` and `next_b` either `-1` or a row number?
   - Does `build(table, 0)` use every row exactly once? Compare its numbers of
     steps and outcomes (piece 6) with the table's.
6. **Tree functions** ([Class 25](/class/25)), each tested on your small conversation from
   piece 2:
   - `count_outcomes`;
   - `count_steps`;
   - `longest`, the most steps on any path to an outcome;
   - `outcomes`, the outcomes' texts, as a list, those after option a before
     those after option b;
   - `path_to`, the options that lead to an outcome with a given text, as a
     list of the options' labels, or `None` if no outcome has that text.
7. **Run it** (Classes [5](/class/5) and [25](/class/25)). Design `talk`, which runs a conversation: it
   prints a step's text and its two options, asks for one with `input()`, and
   continues with the conversation that option leads to; at an outcome, it
   prints the outcome's text, and gives it back. It has no tests (it waits for
   someone to type), so check it by trying it.
8. **A piece of your own.** A function that does something none of pieces 1–7
   do, designed with the whole recipe. For example, it could draw your whole
   conversation as a picture of its tree, give a shorter conversation without
   one of its branches, or answer a question about your table, such as which
   step has the most to read. Your project must use it, not just define it, and
   its Docstring says what it does for the people using your conversation.
