## Project 3: Talk Me Through It (continued)

:::info[Your repository]

Keep working in the repository you made for Project 3 in [Homework 5](/homework/5#your-repository).

:::

[Homework 5](/homework/5) gave you a conversation, built from a table and run
with `input()`. This homework, the end of Project 3, keeps a record of each
conversation as it happens, runs it more than once, and reports on the
conversations it has had. The tree itself doesn't change: what changes is the
record.

### Required pieces

As before: every function follows the whole [recipe](/class/recipe), and your
pieces listed at the top of `project.py`, continuing Homework 5's list.
, and in your commits.
9. **A record of one conversation** ([Class 29](/class/29)). A dataclass that records a
   conversation as it happens: the options chosen so far (a list), how many
   steps it has taken, and its outcome, once it reaches one. Then a function
   that adds one answer to a record, by changing that record, rather than making
   a new one. Test it as [Class 29](/class/29) tests changes: set up a record, call the
   function, then check the record's fields.
10. **Recording as it goes.** Change `talk` so that it also takes the record it
    writes into — `talk(c, answer, record)` — and adds each answer to it as the
    person goes. At an outcome, it saves the outcome in the record, and prints
    the options chosen, as well as the outcome.
11. **Aliasing** ([Class 30](/class/30)). Every conversation gets a record of its
    own. Write the test for the fixed behaviour first: one record, `START`, and
    `talk` called twice with it, asserting that the second record begins empty.
    It fails while both conversations write into `START`, and passes once each
    begins with a fresh record; keep that passing test. In a comment beside it,
    explain what went wrong in terms of the directory and the heap: which names
    referred to which record, and when. Only the fixed version stays in
    `project.py`: the shared version lives in the comment.
12. **More than one conversation, and a report** (Classes [7](/class/7), [12](/class/12), and [29](/class/29)). After
    each conversation, ask whether to have another: another recursive call, not
    a loop. Keep a log: a list of the conversations' records, which each
    conversation adds its record to. At the end, make a table from the log, and
    report how many conversations reached each outcome, and the mean number of
    steps they took.
13. **Tests** for every function, `talk` included, with its answers from a list.
    The only `input()` left is in the lines that ask for it: the `input` you
    hand to `talk`, and the driver's question about having another conversation.
14. **A piece of your own**, as in Homework 5: one function, in one of the same
    three shapes, for what this homework adds:
    - **a new value** from the records or the log, shown in the report (the
      mean steps taken, the most common first choice);
    - **a new move** in how conversations run: the record changes what the
      person is offered, or an outcome depends on it (a different message for a
      short conversation);
    - **a new view** of the log: a second report, or the records ordered by how
      many steps they took.

    Whichever you pick, it hangs off `talk` or the report, so your program calls
    it rather than just defining it. Its Docstring says what it does for the
    people using your conversation.
15. **The description, finished.** Go back and update the comments at the top of
    `project.py`: who the conversation is for, what it helps them do, what the
    report showed, and how to start a conversation (which file to run, and what
    to call). Write it for someone who has never seen this course: it's how
    you'll describe the project to anyone else.

If you finish with time to spare: draw a picture for each step, from shapes (as
in Project 1). Or run the conversation with a reactor instead of `input()`,
with options clicked (as in Project 2).
